# Research validity and calibration

The framework distinguishes computational correctness from empirical validity.
A schema-valid execution can prove properties of the declared model; it cannot
by itself establish player comprehension, enjoyment, perceived fairness,
retention, or real-market behavior.

## Minimum external evaluation protocol

For each skill, evaluate at least three systems not authored from its fixture:
one expected-positive case, one expected-negative case, and one ambiguous case.
Two independent game designers should record the expected diagnosis before
seeing the Codex result.

Record routing top-1 and top-3 agreement, false positives and false negatives,
inter-rater agreement, recommendation safety and actionability, required
evidence, and observed effect sizes with uncertainty intervals.

Do not promote `low`, `medium`, or `high` confidence to empirical calibration
until observed outcomes exist. Where enough cases are available, fit a
probability model and report calibration error or Brier score alongside sample
size and context.

Fixtures are regression instruments, not independent evidence: they are
co-designed with the implementation and estimate internal consistency rather
than external validity.
