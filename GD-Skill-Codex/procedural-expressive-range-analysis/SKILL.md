## Theoretical Context and System Function

This skill applies the formal *Expressive Range Analysis* (ERA) methodology, developed by Gillian Smith and Jim Whitehead, to evaluate and diagnose Procedural Content Generation (PCG) algorithms. Instead of evaluating isolated instances generated by an algorithm, ERA analyzes the geometry and density of the possibility space produced by thousands of executions under distinct *seeds*.

The generator's expressive space is delimited by at least two Behavioral Characterizations / quantitative metrics ($BC_1$ and $BC_2$). The two-dimensional probability distribution over the Cartesian space is defined by:

$$P(BC_1, BC_2) = \frac{n(BC_1, BC_2)}{N}$$

where $n(BC_1, BC_2)$ is the number of generated artifacts whose metrics fall within the continuous interval of coordinates $(BC_1, BC_2)$, and $N$ is the total number of generated samples ($N \ge 1000$).

The objective of the agent is to quantify generator bias, identify unfilled design space gaps, and provide parameter restructuring recommendations.

## 1. Integrated Processing Protocol

When activating this skill, the agent must sequentially execute the following technical analysis stages:

### Stage 1: Behavioral Characterization Definition ($BC$)

1. Select two orthogonal quantitative metrics $BC_1$ and $BC_2$ with normalized values $BC \in [0, 1]$:
   - **Topological / Structural Metrics:** (e.g., $BC_1 = \text{Linearity}$, where $0$ indicates fully branched/mesh graph and $1$ indicates single straight path).
   - **Challenge Density Metrics:** (e.g., $BC_2 = \text{Enemy Density}$, or hazard occupancy rate per square meter).
   - **Leniency / Difficulty Metrics:** Metrics measuring estimated resource cost to traverse the level.

### Stage 2: Massive Sampling and Expressive Space Collection

1. Instantiate $N$ simulations of the generation algorithm ($N \ge 1000$) varying the stochastic seed.
2. For each generated artifact $a_i$ ($i = 1, \dots, N$), extract metric coordinates $(BC_1(a_i), BC_2(a_i))$.

### Stage 3: Density Mapping and Statistical Analysis

1. Discretize the Cartesian plane $[0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ into a $k \times k$ bin grid (typically $10 \times 10$ or $20 \times 20$).
2. Calculate formal statistical metrics of distribution $P(BC_1, BC_2)$:
   - **Expressive Space Coverage ($E_{\text{fill}}$):** Percentage of bins containing at least one sample ($n > 0$).
   - **Spatial Entropy ($H_{\text{spatial}}$):** Degree of uniformity of instance distribution over the plane:

$$H_{\text{spatial}} = -\sum_{i=1}^{k^2} p_i \ln(p_i)$$

   - **Maximum Density Point / Primary Bias ($BC_{\text{bias}}$):** Coordinates of the bin with highest sample concentration.

### Stage 4: Playability and Solvability Limit Assessment

1. Map the region of **Logical Validity ($V_{\text{valid}} \subset [0, 1]^2$)**, delimiting the subarea where generated levels provably meet formal playability requirements.

---

## 2. Algorithmic Detection of Generation Anomalies

The agent must analyze the expressive space density map and flag the following algorithmic failures:

### A. Extreme Algorithmic Bias / Content Homogeneity

$$H_{\text{spatial}} \ll H_{\text{max}} \quad \land \quad \max(P(BC_1, BC_2)) > 0.50$$

Action: Identify when more than 50% of generated levels concentrate in a single narrow region of expressive space, making procedural runs repetitive.

### B. Unintentional Design Space Gaps

$$\exists \text{Region } R \subset V_{\text{valid}} \quad \text{such that} \quad P(BC_1, BC_2 \in R) = 0$$

Action: Flag theoretically valid and desirable regions that the algorithm cannot instantiate due to incorrect hard constraints in code.

### C. Generation of Unsolvable Artifacts / Outliers

$$\exists a_i \quad \text{where} \quad (BC_1(a_i), BC_2(a_i)) \notin V_{\text{valid}}$$

Action: Alert about procedural samples generated outside solvability bounds (e.g., maps without critical path to exit or with unsustainable threat density).

## Mathematical Status

### Formal Guarantees
Coverage and entropy measure distribution, not quality; diversity counts only outputs passing validity and quality gates.

### Derived Metrics
Derived metrics include valid coverage, entropy, pairwise distance and generator bias.

### Heuristics and Design Judgments
Heuristics judge meaningful variation.

### Required Simulation or Playtesting
Required simulation or playtesting: seeded samples, expert quality scoring and player recognition tests.

## Hypotheses and Limitations

- Assumes selected behavioral characterizations are measurable, sufficiently independent, and normalized consistently.
- Coverage and entropy estimates depend on sample size and binning; they do not by themselves establish playability or design quality.

## 4. Procedure Execution Example

If the user requests: *"Evaluate a roguelike room-based map generator where $BC_1$ is graph non-linearity and $BC_2$ is trap density per square meter."*

The agent applying this skill formalizes:

- $BC_1 = \text{Number of Graph Cycles} / \text{Maximum Possible Cycles}$.
- $BC_2 = \text{Number of Traps} / \text{Total Terrain Area}$.
- Runs simulation with $N = 1000$ procedural samples.
- **Results:**
  - $E_{\text{fill}} = 31\%$ (Restricted expressive space).
  - $BC_{\text{bias}} = (0.15, 0.80)$ with $62\%$ of samples concentrated in this area.
- **Diagnosis:** Algorithm is highly biased towards strictly linear maps with extremely high trap density.
- **Detected Anomaly:** Region $(BC_1 > 0.60, BC_2 < 0.30)$ has $0\%$ density. Generator incapable of producing exploratory maps with low hazard presence.
- **Code Recommendation:** Relax edge deallocation constraint in Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) algorithm to allow deliberate creation of shortcuts and cycles in the graph.
